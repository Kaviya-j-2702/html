About this report
How to read {{ student.first_name }}'s results

Every score in this report comes directly from {{ student.first_name }}'s own Multiple Intelligence assessment. Nothing here is generic or reused across students — the structure and depth of each section is shaped by their actual results.

What is Multiple Intelligence theory?

Developed by Harvard psychologist Dr. Howard Gardner in 1983, Multiple Intelligence theory proposes that people don't have one single kind of intelligence, but several distinct ones — each reflecting a different way of thinking, learning and solving problems. {{ brand.name }}'s {{ brand.program_name }} measures seven of these intelligences through hands-on activities, not written tests alone.

This is a measure of innate ability

This assessment measures innate ability — the natural, inborn shape of how {{ student.first_name }} thinks and solves problems — not how much a particular skill has been taught or practiced. A lower score means an intelligence is less natural right now, not that they failed to try or weren't exposed to it. This report always shows scaled, sub-skill-level scores — never a single blended number for an intelligence — so no signal is lost to averaging.

How results are grouped in this report

To make sense of several intelligences and many individual scores at once, this report groups {{ student.first_name }}'s results into three tiers, based on their own internal average per intelligence. These tiers are relative to their own profile only — not a comparison against other students.

Standout
The clearest natural signal in the results this round.
Developing
Present and usable, without being the dominant natural mode.
Growth area
Not where natural strengths lie; building it takes deliberate effort.
